“New Habits, New Harms: Pediatric Dentists and Substance-Related Oral Changes in Adolescents.” Thank you again for your assistance with the statistical analysis of our survey data. Before outlining the analyses, I wanted to clarify how respondents should be grouped. The survey was distributed through two separate SurveyMonkey collectors associated with different AAPD mailing lists: “Pediatric Dentist Survey”: Practicing Pediatric Dentists “Resident Survey”: Pediatric Dental Residents The SurveyMonkey collector should therefore serve as the primary grouping variable, distinguishing practicing pediatric dentists from pediatric dental residents. Question 3 asked respondents to indicate their years in practice or residency year. Some residents selected “less than 5 years” rather than identifying their residency year because of this we do not want residency year analyzed as a variable. Question 3 should only be used to analyze years in practice among practicing pediatric dentists. Additionally in regard to question 2 of the resident survey, six respondents indicated that their current practice setting is private practice and one respondent indicated that they are a university faculty. It is likely that these respondents responded to the survey after graduating from their residency program. These respondents should still be classified as residents based on the collector through which they responded. Our primary objective is to compare practicing pediatric dentists and pediatric dental residents with respect to comfort, clinical practices, knowledge of AAPD guidelines, clinical knowledge, and educational experiences related to substance-associated oral findings in adolescents.
